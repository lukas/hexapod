# Programmed alpha/speed comparison: completed real-robot test

The robot completed all four eight-second walks once and returned to limp. This was programmed gait 1, not RL. The controlled comparison does not support lowering alpha as a general cure for shaking: the two alpha settings were close at 30 mm/s, while high alpha had less rapid body image motion at 40 mm/s in the matched clean interval.

| Commanded speed | Alpha | Rapid body image-motion RMS | Raw host command rate | Longest host command gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|30 mm/s|0.25|4.70 px|98.04 Hz|20.93 ms|
|30 mm/s|1.00|4.97 px|97.52 Hz|21.49 ms|
|40 mm/s|0.25|9.98 px|97.40 Hz|28.24 ms|
|40 mm/s|1.00|5.46 px|97.47 Hz|23.34 ms|

The image metric uses chassis tag 0, unique capture timestamps, an identical 1.5–6 Hz filter and the same clean 4.5–7.5 s window in every trial. Fixed carpet optical flow is an independent camera/noise control; its held-out residual p95 is below 0.24 px. Quiet holds measured roughly 0.03–0.065 px RMS. At30 mm/s the high/low difference is only 6%, which is not a clear alpha effect from one trial. At40 mm/s the high-alpha cell is about 45% lower in this interval. Changing the filter to 2–5 Hz retains the ordering. Raising speed increases the low-alpha metric markedly but only modestly changes the high-alpha metric, so neither alpha nor speed alone explains all behavior.

The video shows gait-activated surges and rocking with broad band-limited frequency bins around 1.7–2.7 Hz. The three-second measurement window gives only 0.33 Hz frequency resolution, and intended gait harmonics overlap this band. This is not an identified mechanical resonance. Pixels include translation, body rocking and yaw; they are not calibrated millimetres or a direct measurement of joint flex, foot slip or contact force. One 8 s trial per cell, fixed order and the short clean window limit causal/general conclusions. No measured travel speed or MuJoCo equivalence is claimed.

The low-alpha 40 recording has a large exposure/lighting change around 2.3–3.6 s that temporarily damages tag/background tracking. The common clean window excludes it; a longer-gap sensitivity check preserves the alpha ordering. The initial per-frame homography from tiny floor-tag corners exaggerated motion and invalidated many frames. Those preliminary metrics are explicitly superseded by the direct chassis tracking and background control, not used for this result. The video is retained in full, including the lighting change.

All cells used the same gait 1 period 3.2 s, lift 28mm,100 Hz compute and servo profile 2000/80, with alpha/speed as the intended changes. The first STEP walk-ready preflight was within2 degrees of nominal. Actual command packets were valid 18-goal W writes at 97.4–98.0 Hz, always 2000/80. The roughly 47–48 Hz decoded sync_write trace is logging-rate-limited and is not the physical write rate. Raw serial_tx measures host-to-MCU transmission, not direct servo receipt. Complete duplicate packets occurred about 11–12% in every cell; high alpha reduced per-joint duplicates. Neither command cadence nor duplicates explain an alpha-specific shaking effect on their own.

Every sampled encoder set contained 18IDs. Recorded exchanges and communication capture reported no failures or drops. Peak sampled per-servo currents were 0.260/0.455/0.520/0.501 A in execution order (low30, high30, high40, low-alpha 40), with minimum reported voltage 11.1–11.2V. Typical temperatures were 28–33 C. Isolated impossible temperature jumps, including 31→142→31 C within a second, are retained as telemetry anomalies rather than reported as actual sustained heating. Sparse feedback cannot resolve the shaking frequency or establish a joint-level cause. The planned STEP lower recorded 2.81 A peak; final snapshot/telemetry and a later fresh gallery view show the robot down, limp, with all 18 motors healthy and IMU healthy.

The useful next intervention is a single matched servo-acceleration comparison at a fixed high-alpha 40 mm/s condition: retain 100 Hz, gait geometry and speed limit 2000, vary acceleration 80→20, and compare body surges together with tracking lag/clearance. This is a proposed diagnostic change, not a proven fix or a claim that lower acceleration will win. Do not lower the control rate or rewrite RL training on the evidence from this test. The earlier good fluid 9/14 footage also showed real gait-related shaking; those presets have their own timing and do not expose this gait 1 alpha knob, so this result is not an alpha-only verdict for every programmed gait. No additional physical/offline experiment is queued by this result.

## Recording recovery and code

The native recording supplied approximately 24–25.5 fresh images/s during the walks. Its MP4 was truncated at exactly 64 MiB because the worker's transcript limit was inherited by video-writing children as RLIMIT_FSIZE. The original runner incorrectly reported ok=true/camera_error=null after silent VideoWriter write failures. Original camera timestamps, run_summary.json and truncated bytes are preserved unchanged; the truncated MP4 is archived as camera_raw_truncated.mp4.bin so it is not offered as a playable video.

Recovery reparsed the complete H264 pictures and reconstructed 4577 verified presentation frames. Picture order is contiguous, and four independent before-condition snapshots align with their expected source frames at zero offset. Every walking segment survives. The recovered video contains 152.566667 s; final 202 frames(6.733 s), including the end of lowering, were not in the truncated source. They are not invented or represented as recovered. Final stills and telemetry provide the terminal limp evidence. The side-by-side eight-second montage uses original capture timestamps at real time. Recovery details, checksums and frame mappings are in recovery_manifest.json, picture_order_audit.json and snapshot_alignment.json.
