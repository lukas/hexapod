# L4/L5 joint-flex results — 2026-09-02

This is the durable summary of the first powered comparison on `hexapod-1`.
Raw datasets and camera recordings stay in the ignored `sysid/datasets/` and
`artifacts/joint_flex/` trees. Protocols, generators, analysis helpers, and this
summary are versioned so the work can be reproduced.

## Test configuration

- The chassis was supported and the operator reported no crack or changed
  fastener before testing.
- Robot telemetry came from the position-feedback API.
- Initial L4 tests used camera 0 on hip-side tag 31 and the iPhone view on
  knee-side tag 25. The completed larger ladder, creep, and cycle tests used
  camera 0, camera 1, and the MacBook camera; all three tracked tag 31.
- During L4 capture, adjacent L3/L5 yaw joints eased to -35/+35 degrees to
  expose the side tags, then returned to zero. The calculated minimum adjacent
  centerline clearance was 99.62 mm.
- All completed runs ended limp. No run aborted, clamped a target, or recorded
  a sample overrun. Peak reported current was 0.104 A across the L4 series.
- Force was not measured, so the results quantify repeatable command/response
  hysteresis rather than absolute stiffness in N-m/degree.

## Air-control comparison

The air-control protocol isolates unloaded servo/output/geartrain behavior.
Mean encoder loop magnitude was:

| Leg | Hip | Knee |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| L5 | 0.703 deg | 0.410 deg |
| L4 | 0.117 deg | 0.059 deg |

L5 therefore has a large unloaded deadband/hysteresis component that is not
present at the same scale on L4. L4 camera loops were small: 0.165 deg on
camera 0 and 0.146 deg on the complementary iPhone line measurement.

## Planted micro-ladder comparison

Five load/unload cycles were run at each radial command amplitude. Values below
are mean absolute encoder loop magnitudes.

| Command | L5 hip | L5 knee | L4 hip | L4 knee |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.5 mm | 0.000 deg | 0.000 deg | 0.000 deg | 0.000 deg |
| 1.0 mm | 0.106 deg | 0.106 deg | 0.106 deg | 0.158 deg |
| 2.0 mm | 0.158 deg | 0.211 deg | 0.158 deg | 0.351 deg |
| 3.0 mm | 0.194 deg | 0.176 deg | 0.176 deg | 0.334 deg |

For L4, both independent camera views also show a response onset around 2 mm.
Camera 0 line-loop magnitude rose from 0.098 deg at 1 mm to 0.329 deg at 2 mm;
the iPhone line rose from 0.002 deg to 0.187 deg.

## Planted amplitude ladder

Three load/unload cycles were run at each larger radial command. Mean absolute
encoder loop magnitude was:

| Command | L5 hip | L5 knee | L4 hip | L4 knee |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3.75 mm | 0.732 deg | 0.674 deg | 0.293 deg | 0.352 deg |
| 7.5 mm | 1.026 deg | 0.908 deg | 0.176 deg | 0.235 deg |
| 11.25 mm | 1.142 deg | 0.938 deg | 0.205 deg | 0.146 deg |
| 15 mm | 1.143 deg | 0.937 deg | 0.176 deg | 0.263 deg |

L5 grows to roughly 1 degree of planted hysteresis, while L4 stays below
0.36 degrees and does not grow monotonically with amplitude. Three L4 visual
projections corroborated the small loop scale. Depending on view, their mean
tag-31-to-tag-11 line loops were approximately 0.11-0.35 degrees on camera 0,
0.16-0.29 degrees on camera 1, and 0.16-0.32 degrees on the MacBook camera.

## Creep and loaded-cycle repeatability

During a 90-second fixed-command planted hold, L4 moved by one hip encoder
count (0.088 degrees) and zero knee counts. Both joints were unchanged through
118 seconds of unloaded recovery. The camera views showed no consistent slow
recovery trend. L5 likewise had zero encoder-count drift in its matching test.

The five-cycle vertical triangle test measured an equal-command moving loop of
5.062 +/- 0.561 degrees at the L4 hip and 0.370 +/- 0.096 degrees at its knee.
The L5 result was larger: 6.644 +/- 0.934 degrees hip and 0.510 +/- 0.039
degrees knee. Camera 0 and camera 1 measured 5.12/5.13-degree L4 line loops;
the MacBook view measured 6.22 degrees because of its different projection.
These moving-loop values include servo tracking lag and must not be interpreted
as static structural flex. The ground-pose residual from cycle 1 to cycle 5 was
only +0.088 degrees hip and -0.176 degrees knee on L4.

## Current interpretation

The data supports two components:

1. L5 has substantial unloaded servo/output/geartrain deadband, because its air
   loop is roughly six to seven times L4's at both measured joints.
2. The larger planted ladder strengthens the L5 diagnosis: L5 reaches
   0.7-1.1 degrees while L4 remains at 0.15-0.35 degrees. The L4 knee-dominant
   response at 2-3 mm is therefore a small contact-onset effect, not evidence
   that L4 is generally looser.
3. Neither leg shows meaningful time-dependent encoder creep at the tested
   fixed command. Progressive creep is not supported at this load and duration.

This does not yet uniquely localize the planted component to a fastener, yoke,
printed link, bearing stack, or servo mount. The camera and encoder measurements
establish the onset and joint distribution, but force calibration and more
local markers are required for an absolute or component-level stiffness claim.
The relative diagnosis is complete: L5 is the abnormal leg, with a dominant
unloaded deadband component and additional planted hysteresis.
